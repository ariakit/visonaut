## Projections, with exact assumptions

- Applying only the _observed mean_ to the separate 10,580 design target gives `10,580 × 62,898,893 / 3,582 = 185,781,767` original bytes (185.8 MB). Applying it to the 35,820 tenfold stress target gives 628,988,930 bytes (629.0 MB). These are original-byte sensitivities, not measurements of 10,580 or 35,820 fresh captures. At 40,000 captures, the same mean gives 702,388,532 bytes. Duplicate bytes may lower actual uploads; changed image size distributions may raise them.
- The current per-image limit of 2 MiB is 9.98 times the observed maximum encoded PNG. The current 2.1-million-pixel decoder limit is only 1.36 times the observed maximum pixel count. At 40,000 captures, per-image byte limits alone would allow 83,886,080,000 declared original bytes (83.9 GB), before derived images, staging, and backups. The current service also enforces 512 MiB per shard and 2 GiB aggregate declared original bytes per run. The capture-count cap alone cannot serve as a per-run storage or cost cap.
- If the fixed 2.378004765 GB inventory remains reused and 30 SQL roots preserve 6.138912930 GB, their combined retained bytes are 8.516917695 GB. At 60 SQL roots and 12.277825860 GB SQL, the combined fixed-inventory sensitivity is 14.655830625 GB. This treats all those bytes as present for a full 30-day billing month, for a conservative steady-state storage estimate. It excludes live R2, staging, exports, changed groups, and other shared-account storage.
- One cold plus 59 warm backups on that exact fixed inventory yields 75,460 recorded Class A calls and 229,571 Class B calls. It does not predict that future backups will be warm, finish at the measured pace, or meet 24-hour RPO.

## Official Cloudflare pricing and rate equivalents

Use the [R2 Standard pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/) of $0.015/GB-month, $4.50/million Class A calls, and $0.36/million Class B calls. The shared paid account includes 10 GB-month, 1 million Class A, and 10 million Class B per month. R2 rounds billable usage to the next billing unit. The fixed 60-root inventory's unrounded all-marginal storage rate is `$0.015 × 14.655830625 = $0.21984/month`. In a hypothetical otherwise unused account, 10 GB included and 5 rounded GB billable would be $0.075; with no unused allowance, 15 rounded GB would be $0.225. The one-cold-plus-59-warm application calls have unrounded all-marginal rate equivalents of $0.33957 Class A and $0.08265 Class B. They exclude unlogged staging and controller activity and cannot be used as an invoice.

At [D1 paid rates](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/platform/pricing/), the frozen archive's recorded 421,489,106 reads and 141,749 writes have unrounded all-marginal rate equivalents of $0.42149 and $0.14175. D1 includes 25 billion reads, 50 million writes, and 5 GB storage per month across the paid account. Current-source query shape and monthly frequency are unknown.

[Queues](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/platform/pricing/) charge $0.40/million operations after 1 million included per month; a successful sub-64-KB message normally costs one write, read, and delete. Exactly one successful comparison message per 35,820 captures would be 107,460 operations, or $0.042984 at the unrounded all-marginal rate. Retries, dead letters, backup work, and larger messages add operations. [Workers Standard](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/) includes 10 million requests and 30 million CPU milliseconds per paid-account month, then $0.30/million requests and $0.02/million CPU milliseconds. At 35,820 comparisons, every _additional measured_ 100 ms of Worker CPU per comparison would be 3,582,000 CPU ms, or $0.07164 at the unrounded all-marginal rate. The real final-source CPU per Ariakit comparison is not measured, so no total Worker cost is stated.

The [Container basic instance](https://developers.cloudflare.com/containers/platform/pricing/) provisions 1 GiB memory and 4 GB disk. Memory and disk rates are $0.0000025/GiB-second and $0.00000007/GB-second; active CPU is $0.000020/vCPU-second. One instance running continuously for 30 days would incur $7.20576 in memory and disk at overage rates before monthly included usage, or $6.93036 if the full 25 GiB-hours of memory and 200 GB-hours of disk included usage were still available. These are always-on sensitivities, not measured Visonaut monthly costs. The Container has its own [Durable Object request/duration charges](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/pricing/), and logs or network egress can add charges. Production and preview currently select the Worker codec; diagnostic Container duty cycle does not establish a production fallback bill.

For every dimension, actual incremental shared-account cost must use `C(account usage + Visonaut usage) - C(account usage)`, applying its included allotment and rounding. The account usage baseline, final-source production usage, retries, changed-image rate, and backup group churn are missing. Do not add the preceding rate equivalents and label the sum a bill.

## Recommended provisional admission policy

Retain the deployed 40,000-capture/run ceiling, 2 MiB encoded-image ceiling, 2.1-million-pixel decoder ceiling, 512 MiB/shard ceiling, and at most two active upload/comparison runs. The 40,000 count leaves 4,180 captures (11.67%) over the separate 35,820 stress count; it is not a hosted 40,000-capture pass. Continue warning at 512 MiB physical D1 and 384 MiB latest completed SQL; refuse new run identities at 768 MiB physical D1 or 512 MiB SQL, while allowing existing work to finish. Keep the measured backup runtime bounds: 2 GiB maximum SQL, 420-second export/download deadline, 240-second queue CPU, and 720-second lease. These are protective gates, not throughput guarantees. See [current limits](../../apps/web/wrangler.jsonc), [capacity admission](../../apps/web/src/capacity.ts), and [runtime bounds](./runtime-bounds/README.md).

Retain the provisional **2 GiB original bytes per run** limit across all shards. The implementation and focused tests are recorded in the current source; this remains a provisional budget, not a full workload pass. It is 3.41 times the 35,820-count projection at the observed mean and 1.31 times `40,000 × observed p95 bytes`; it closes the gap between a 40,000-count cap and the theoretical 83.9 GB allowed by per-image checks. The service checks aggregate declared bytes atomically and handles exact inherited originals without double-counting. Validate the chosen threshold with the actual Ariakit upload and comparison path before claiming it supports the target. Keep the shard ceiling so one upload remains bounded.

The workflow-owned staging path adds a provisional **8 GiB aggregate cap** on declared original bytes in live attempts that have not sealed a complete service run. This includes attempts with an unsealed service run after failed materialization. The cap is four maximum-size 2 GiB runs, or more than twelve runs at the separate 628,988,930-byte projection. The service checks the aggregate and per-run capture count in the manifest declaration transaction. A retry of an identical manifest adds no reservation. After five consecutive missing-or-corrupt-original reconciliation failures, an incomplete service run moves to failed state and stops using one of the two active-run slots; its App check queues a failure and cannot pass, and in-app attention remains. Its staged bytes still count against the 8 GiB cap while the stage is live. Inherited rerun attempts keep their source originals until the 70-day staged-retention boundary. Materialization stops renewing its writer lease at that boundary; after the last lease expires, bounded cleanup closes any still-active unsealed run, removes its staged child rows, and releases its byte reservation. If the queued GitHub failure has not been delivered by then, the required check stays pending and fail-closed while the in-app alert stays unresolved; cleanup does not record it as delivered. At the published R2 Standard storage rate, 8 GiB is 8.589934592 decimal GB and has an unrounded all-marginal sensitivity of **$0.12885/month if continuously full**. This is not a bill or a monthly cost ceiling: it excludes object operations, backups, D1, and shared allowances. Measure actual stage duration and bytes before revising the cap.

Materialized attempts also retain their own canonical originals for the 70-day GitHub rerun window, even if the service run closes and its detailed history is archived after 30 days. A newer live, submitted attempt keeps a matching source pinned until it seals or reaches its own 70-day staging boundary. This bounded dependency can hold the source beyond its original 70-day boundary. At expiry, bounded cleanup removes the staging rows and releases the byte pin; normal closed-run retention may then remove the originals. The original bytes of sealed materialized runs and the temporary D1 staging rows are **outside the 8 GiB staging cap**; incomplete unsealed materialized runs remain inside it until cleanup. Each maximum-size sealed materialized run can add up to 2 GiB of original bytes, or $0.03221/month at the unrounded all-marginal R2 Standard storage rate if those bytes remain for a full 30-day month. The number of such runs, their actual sizes, dependency extensions, and derived/backup bytes are not yet measured, so the 8 GiB sensitivity cannot be used as an overall storage-cost ceiling.

The current E08 backup inventory covers materialized run images and their manifest evidence. It does not include a submitted attempt that has not materialized. After a restore, staged SQL may therefore refer to missing R2 objects. Reconciliation checks those bytes and cannot pass the App check when they are absent. Recovery requires a fresh full signed upload rather than a failed-job-only rerun that inherits the missing attempt. If materialization already reserved a service run, five consecutive missing-or-corrupt-original reconciliation failures move it to failed state, free its active-run slot, stop its retries, and retain in-app attention. Other reconciliation errors still move to hourly retries after five failures; a later successful pass clears their alert. The earlier E08 drill did not measure this recovery case. Treat staged-only uploads as recoverable in-flight work, not as restored completed review history.

No numeric monthly **incremental USD ceiling** can yet be selected as evidence-backed. Keep the launch cost gate open until production-equivalent Workers/Queue/D1/R2/Container meter populations, retained bytes, backup churn, run frequency, and a comparable shared-account counterfactual are recorded. The $102.00 account-wide D1 overage, driven by one-off fixtures, and $0.98 shared R2 storage charge cannot supply that ceiling. A provisional spend alert would be an operational preference, not a proven budget or launch pass.
