# E07 admission and cost model (read-only, September 23, 2026)

Scope: Ariakit issue #1. These are measured observations, rate-equivalent sensitivities, and a provisional admission recommendation. The frozen large fixture and small synthetic Container diagnostic do not certify final-source hosted throughput or a monthly Visonaut bill. The meter queries did not change Cloudflare resources.

## Measured inputs

- The current desktop `@visual` suite produced 3,582 selected captures per pass (1,230 Chromium, 1,230 Firefox, 1,122 WebKit); three complete passes produced 10,746 selected captures. One pass contained 62,898,893 PNG bytes (17,559.71 bytes mean; 41,137 p95; 210,157 max) and 510,234,282 pixels (142,443.96 mean; 1,547,520 max). Four Firefox 120-second failed attempts were recovered across the three passes. See [capture measurement](./ariakit-capture-measurement/README.md) and [source count](../../packages/compare/evidence/ariakit-capture-count.md).
- A local 35,820-capture review fixture measured list p95 65.4 ms, first current-image p95 112.4 ms, and cached next-variant p95 34.4 ms after the model was available. Its generated PNGs and localhost network do not represent deployed image delivery. See [review-scale record](./review-scale/README.md).
- A frozen hosted v3 large fixture contained 75,195 backup-inventory objects and 2,378,004,765 bytes across seven immutable groups. Its cold and warm SQL exports were 204,623,590 and 204,630,431 bytes, respectively. The cold backup completed in 5,772.659 seconds including failure/restart; the warm backup took 115.555 seconds. Cold application receipts counted 75,283 R2 Class A calls and 229,394 Class B calls; warm receipts counted three of each. A separate archive counted 421,489,106 D1 reads and 141,749 writes. These are application receipts, not provider billing meters; the fixture repeats historical image bytes and predates final packing/cadence changes. See [large v3 record](./v3-large-numeric/README.md).
- A separate 5,919,244,288-byte physical D1 stress database exported 3,860,404,222 SQL bytes. Its export/download observation exceeded the selected 420-second deadline, and SQL exceeded the selected 2 GiB automatic-backup limit. The D1-only restore is not a full image-plus-database recovery. See [D1 capacity restore](./d1-capacity-restore.md).
- The final-image two-card Container diagnostic sampled up to 167,440,384 bytes in its red-card metric minute. Two usage groups recorded 96,637,054,530 memory byte-seconds, 360,001,081,712 disk byte-seconds, and 0.72 active CPU seconds. These include startup/idle and cannot be assigned to a single comparison. Neither sampled memory nor an inactive instance establishes full-matrix peak, latency, or duty cycle. See [integrated Container record](./container-codec/hosted-queue.md).

## Projections, with exact assumptions

- Applying only the _observed mean_ to the separate 10,580 design target gives `10,580 × 62,898,893 / 3,582 = 185,781,767` original bytes (185.8 MB). Applying it to the 35,820 tenfold stress target gives 628,988,930 bytes (629.0 MB). These are original-byte sensitivities, not measurements of 10,580 or 35,820 fresh captures. At 40,000 captures, the same mean gives 702,388,532 bytes. Duplicate bytes may lower actual uploads; changed image size distributions may raise them.
- The current per-image limit of 2 MiB is 9.98 times the observed maximum encoded PNG. The current 2.1-million-pixel decoder limit is only 1.36 times the observed maximum pixel count. At 40,000 captures, per-image byte limits alone allow 83,886,080,000 declared original bytes (83.9 GB), before derived images, staging, and backups. The 512 MiB cap applies to each shard, and the service has no explicit aggregate original-byte cap across shards. Thus the capture-count cap cannot serve as a per-run storage or cost cap.
- If the fixed 2.378004765 GB inventory remains reused and 30 SQL roots preserve 6.138912930 GB, their combined retained bytes are 8.516917695 GB. At 60 SQL roots and 12.277825860 GB SQL, the combined fixed-inventory sensitivity is 14.655830625 GB. This treats all those bytes as present for a full 30-day billing month, for a conservative steady-state storage estimate. It excludes live R2, staging, exports, changed groups, and other shared-account storage.
- One cold plus 59 warm backups on that exact fixed inventory yields 75,460 recorded Class A calls and 229,571 Class B calls. It does not predict that future backups will be warm, finish at the measured pace, or meet 24-hour RPO.

## Official Cloudflare pricing and rate equivalents

Use the [R2 Standard pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/) of $0.015/GB-month, $4.50/million Class A calls, and $0.36/million Class B calls. The shared paid account includes 10 GB-month, 1 million Class A, and 10 million Class B per month. R2 rounds billable usage to the next billing unit. The fixed 60-root inventory's unrounded all-marginal storage rate is `$0.015 × 14.655830625 = $0.21984/month`. In a hypothetical otherwise unused account, 10 GB included and 5 rounded GB billable would be $0.075; with no unused allowance, 15 rounded GB would be $0.225. The one-cold-plus-59-warm application calls have unrounded all-marginal rate equivalents of $0.33957 Class A and $0.08265 Class B. They exclude unlogged staging and controller activity and cannot be used as an invoice.

At [D1 paid rates](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/platform/pricing/), the frozen archive's recorded 421,489,106 reads and 141,749 writes have unrounded all-marginal rate equivalents of $0.42149 and $0.14175. D1 includes 25 billion reads, 50 million writes, and 5 GB storage per month across the paid account. Current-source query shape and monthly frequency are unknown.

[Queues](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/platform/pricing/) charge $0.40/million operations after 1 million included per month; a successful sub-64-KB message normally costs one write, read, and delete. Exactly one successful comparison message per 35,820 captures would be 107,460 operations, or $0.042984 at the unrounded all-marginal rate. Retries, dead letters, backup work, and larger messages add operations. [Workers Standard](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/) includes 10 million requests and 30 million CPU milliseconds per paid-account month, then $0.30/million requests and $0.02/million CPU milliseconds. At 35,820 comparisons, every _additional measured_ 100 ms of Worker CPU per comparison would be 3,582,000 CPU ms, or $0.07164 at the unrounded all-marginal rate. The real final-source CPU per Ariakit comparison is not measured, so no total Worker cost is stated.

The [Container basic instance](https://developers.cloudflare.com/containers/platform/pricing/) provisions 1 GiB memory and 4 GB disk. Memory and disk rates are $0.0000025/GiB-second and $0.00000007/GB-second; active CPU is $0.000020/vCPU-second. One instance running continuously for 30 days would incur $7.20576 in memory and disk at overage rates before monthly included usage, or $6.93036 if the full 25 GiB-hours of memory and 200 GB-hours of disk included usage were still available. These are always-on sensitivities, not measured Visonaut monthly costs. The Container has its own [Durable Object request/duration charges](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/pricing/), and logs or network egress can add charges. Production and preview currently select the Worker codec; diagnostic Container duty cycle does not establish a production fallback bill.

For every dimension, actual incremental shared-account cost must use `C(account usage + Visonaut usage) - C(account usage)`, applying its included allotment and rounding. The account usage baseline, final-source production usage, retries, changed-image rate, and backup group churn are missing. Do not add the preceding rate equivalents and label the sum a bill.

This observation identifies the meter queries for a future controlled before/after run; it does not establish incremental monthly cost or close E07.

## Recommended provisional admission policy

Retain the deployed 40,000-capture/run ceiling, 2 MiB encoded-image ceiling, 2.1-million-pixel decoder ceiling, 512 MiB/shard ceiling, and at most two active upload/comparison runs. The 40,000 count leaves 4,180 captures (11.67%) over the separate 35,820 stress count; it is not a hosted 40,000-capture pass. Continue warning at 512 MiB physical D1 and 384 MiB latest completed SQL; refuse new run identities at 768 MiB physical D1 or 512 MiB SQL, while allowing existing work to finish. Keep the measured backup runtime bounds: 2 GiB maximum SQL, 420-second export/download deadline, 240-second queue CPU, and 720-second lease. These are protective gates, not throughput guarantees. See [current limits](../../apps/web/wrangler.jsonc), [capacity admission](../../apps/web/src/capacity.ts), and [runtime bounds](./runtime-bounds/README.md).

Enforce a provisional **2 GiB original bytes per run** across all shards before admitting expanded workloads. The implementation and focused tests are recorded in the current source; this measurement remains a provisional budget, not a full workload pass. It is 3.41 times the 35,820-count projection at the observed mean and 1.31 times `40,000 × observed p95 bytes`; it closes the gap between a 40,000-count cap and the theoretical 83.9 GB allowed by per-image checks. Enforce it on trusted plan/manifest declarations and verified original bytes, including retries without double-counting exact inherited originals. Validate the chosen threshold with the actual Ariakit upload and comparison path before claiming it supports the target. Keep the shard ceiling so one upload remains bounded.

No numeric monthly **incremental USD ceiling** can yet be selected or approved as evidence-backed. The recommendation is to leave the launch cost gate open until production-equivalent Workers/Queue/D1/R2/Container meter populations, retained bytes, backup churn, and the shared-account before/after baseline are recorded. A provisional spend alert would be an operational preference, not a proven budget or launch pass.
