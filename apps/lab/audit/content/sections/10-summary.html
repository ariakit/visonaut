How the audit worked

  • 31 areas. One agent audited each area: page load, the run page, sign-in, the database, storage, operations, the API, the packages, CI, and nine parts of the UI. Ten more areas covered gaps that a first review found, for example real production data and other browsers.
  • A second agent for each area. It tried to refute each finding: it opened the cited code, ran the measurements again, and checked each proposed fix against the platform documentation. A third agent checked the real effect of the stronger service findings.
  • Corrected numbers win. Where a check corrected a number or a cause, this document uses the corrected one. The findings table shows the verdict and the final severity of each finding.
  • Labels. A label in front of a claim says if the audit measured it, counted it in the code, calculated it, or assumed it.
  • Production. The audit read production with anonymous GET requests and with your signed-in browser session for the timing of three pages. It wrote nothing. The research for revision r9 and the edits of revisions r10 and r11 sent no request to production.

How to use this document

  • Each section starts with its answer and its numbers. The examples are live: change an input and look at the result.
  • A decision panel has options, and one can have the label Recommended. Each of the 70 panels is settled, and it shows the option that you selected. The label Recommended is not an answer: in 2 of the 12 panels of round 6 it is on another option than the selected one. You can change a selection, write a note, or do both. A changed answer now changes work that is in progress: Feedback and next step says where a note goes.
  • The Decisions menu of the bar at the top lists each decision with its state. Each of the 70 has the state Answered until you clear a selection.
  • The bar at the top counts your answers. Copy continuation prompt gives a text for the next agent session, with only the decisions that you changed.
  • Your answers stay in this browser. They reach an agent only when you paste the prompt.
  • The visual choices are not here. They are in the design lab, which has its own decisions and its own prompt.

Limits. Timing comes from one location (São Paulo) and a small number of samples. Only Chrome ran the full tests; Firefox and WebKit ran in their Playwright builds. No real Safari, no screen reader, and no phone was used. Each section names what nobody verified.
